#### Grading Rubric
- Presentation: 50%
- Participation in discussions: 20%
- Final interactive blog post: 30%

#### Grading Criteria for the Presentation
Each paper is presented by a single student. The presenter should assume that everyone has already read the paper before class.

- Understanding and contextualization (20 points): Does the presenter demonstrate a strong understanding of the paper, its contribution, and how it fits into the broader research area?
- Revibed prototype (30 points): Does the prototype faithfully capture the core interaction and functionality of the original system? Does the presenter reflect on ambiguities, limitations, or assumptions involved in revibing it?
- Analysis (20 points): Does the presenter provide insights beyond summarizing the paper, including connections to related work or broader themes?
- Presentation quality (10 points): Is the presentation clear, well-organized, and engaging?
- Discussion facilitation (20 points): Does the presenter prepare thoughtful prompts and facilitate an engaging and balanced discussion?

#### Grading Criteria for Participation
- The instructor team will keep track of participation during each lecture.
- Participation should demonstrate engagement with the readings and contribute constructively to class discussion.
- The presenter will select five MVP audience members, who each receive one bonus participation point.

#### Grading Criteria for the Final Interactive Blog Post
- Motivation and framing (15 points): Is the research topic clearly motivated and its relevance explained?
- Depth and synthesis (30 points): Does the post demonstrate deep understanding and provide insights beyond summarizing individual papers?
- Interactive artifact (30 points): Does the artifact meaningfully help readers understand or explore the research topic? Is it well integrated into the post?
- Clarity and engagement (15 points): Is the post accessible, coherent, and engaging for a broad computer science audience?
- Writing and sources (10 points): Is the post well-written and supported by appropriate, well-integrated sources?

Bonus — Forward-thinking (+10 points): Does the post offer original or creative perspectives on the future of programming interfaces?
