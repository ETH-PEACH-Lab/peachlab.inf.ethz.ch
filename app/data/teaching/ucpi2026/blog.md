#### What is a blog post?

The final blog post should give any computer science student, even if they are new to the topic, a clear and engaging introduction to the research area.

It should highlight relevant research, include your own perspective, and use your **revibed artifact as part of the explanation**. The artifact should help readers understand, explore, or experience an important idea from the topic.

This is not a paper summary or a literature review. Write in an accessible, conversational style for a broad technical audience.

Examples:

* https://cacm.acm.org/section/blogcacm/
* https://cacm.acm.org/blogcacm/the-evolution-of-user-interface-design/
* https://cacm.acm.org/research/end-user-software-engineering/
* Last year's posts: https://peachlab.inf.ethz.ch/teaching/ucpi2025/

#### Is there a template or structure?

There is no required structure. Please keep the written content around **800–1200 words**, and provide your name and a short bio for the course website.

A strong post should:

* clearly motivate the research topic;
* demonstrate depth and synthesis beyond summarizing individual papers;
* use the interactive artifact meaningfully;
* be clear and engaging for a broad audience;
* integrate and cite relevant sources; and
* optionally offer forward-looking ideas about the future of programming interfaces.

#### Should I find additional papers on the topic?

Yes. Use the seminar readings as a starting point, but explore additional relevant work.

This is a targeted discussion rather than a literature review, so quality matters more than quantity. Please include **at least five substantive sources** and cite them using APA style.
