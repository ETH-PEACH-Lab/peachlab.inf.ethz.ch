Each week, we will focus on one topic in user-centered programming interfaces and read two papers: one from before the rise of LLMs and one from the LLM era. Each paper will be presented by one student.

As part of the presentation, you will also **revibe** the interactive prototype described in your assigned paper.

#### Presentation Format

**1. Background and Related Work ~10 min**

Introduce the problem addressed by the paper and situate the work within the broader research landscape.

Cover:

* What problem does the paper address?
* What was the state of the art at the time?
* What is the key idea or contribution?
* How does the work relate to the broader topic of the week?

**2. Live Demo of the Revibed Prototype ~15 min**

Before class, use contemporary AI-assisted or agentic coding tools to reimplement the interactive prototype described in the paper.

During the presentation, give a live demonstration of your revibed prototype.

Focus on:

* the core interactions and functionality described in the paper;
* how the system works from the user's perspective;
* which parts of the original system you were able to reproduce;
* any ambiguities or missing implementation details you encountered while revibing the system.


**3. Evaluation in the Original Paper ~5 min**

Present how the authors evaluated the original system.

Explain:

* What research questions or hypotheses did the evaluation address?
* What evaluation method did the authors use?
* Who participated, if applicable?
* What tasks, conditions, and measures were used?
* What were the main findings?
* What does the evaluation establish about the system, and what remains uncertain?

**4. Discussion ~15 min**

Lead and moderate the discussion. Prepare a few prompt questions in advance, keep the conversation moving, invite everyone to contribute, and connect the discussion back to the paper, the system, or your revibing experience.
